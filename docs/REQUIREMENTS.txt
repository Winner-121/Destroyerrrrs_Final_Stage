FINAL: COMPUTER VISION FOR THE AUTOMOTIVE BUSINESS AND ROAD SAFETY
==================================================================

Build a product that turns images or video into a decision or an action for
the automotive business or for road safety.

Partners: Toyota Royal Motors, BYD Royal Motors and Ducati.


THE CHALLENGE
-------------

Each finalist team builds a working AI product for its track and defends it as
a business case. There is no dataset and no leaderboard: you choose the
problem, find the data, and prove that the product works, pays off and is hard
to copy.

Choose one mode and name it on your first slide. Both are judged by the same
criteria.

  - Startup: an independent product with its own customers and revenue.
  - Partner product: a product that runs inside a partner company. Name the
    unit that owns it and the process it changes.


EVERY TEAM HANDS IN THREE THINGS
--------------------------------

  1. A prototype that runs end to end, live, including on an input the jury
     gives you.
  2. A deck for the defence.
  3. A repository with the code, a README to run it, and the list of data
     sources with their licences.


TRACK: COMPUTER VISION
----------------------

Partners: Toyota Royal Motors, BYD Royal Motors and Ducati.

Build a product that turns images or video into a decision or an action for
the automotive business or for road safety. The user can be a dealer, a
service centre, an insurer, a fleet owner, a driver or a city.

Directions are examples, not limits:

  - Vehicle inspection: damage and defect detection at handover, trade-in,
    service intake or an insurance claim; appraisal of a used vehicle from
    photos.
  - Dealer and service operations: service-bay and stock-yard analytics, plate
    recognition linked to the customer record, parts identification,
    pre-delivery inspection.
  - Driver and road safety: driver monitoring, motorcycle rider safety, dashcam
    analytics, road-condition mapping, traffic events that continue your
    elimination task.
  - EV and infrastructure: charging-station occupancy and state, parking,
    fleet monitoring.


ENGINEERING CONSTRAINT
----------------------

The product must run in real conditions. State:

  - where inference runs (edge device, server or cloud),
  - the speed on your hardware,
  - the cost per camera or per inspection,
  - how quality holds at night, in rain, and on local vehicles and plates.


DEMO
----

All the web versions of the solutions should be deployed and be publicly
available. Judges (optionally, other contestants) should be able to test it on
their own laptops and/or mobile phones. It is a part of the demo and
presentation, which will be graded.


RULES
-----

No data is provided by the organisers or partners. Both tracks follow the same
rules.

Allowed:

  - Any LLM, coding assistant, open-source model, pretrained weights, public
    dataset or public API. Paid services are at your own expense and must
    appear in your cost model.
  - Data you find, collect or generate yourself, including synthetic data,
    with the source and the generation method declared.
  - Your own elimination-stage code and public libraries, declared in the
    README.

Not allowed:

  - Faked demos: hard-coded answers, mock screens shown as working features,
    invented metrics.
  - Real personal or confidential data: customer records, non-public bank
    data, faces or plates of people who did not consent.
  - Work made before the final, or by people outside the team, presented as
    built during the final.
  - Code, models or ideas of others used without attribution.

The track jury may disqualify a team for any item in the second list. A single
prompt to a general-purpose model behind a UI is not forbidden, but it scores
low on both technology criteria.


DEPLOYMENT AND CODE FREEZE
--------------------------

  - Deploy anywhere that is publicly reachable: a hosting platform (Vercel,
    Netlify, Render, Railway, Hugging Face Spaces...) or your own server and
    domain.
  - Deploy from your submitted repository - frontend and backend. If you
    self-host, we recommend deploying through GitHub Actions so your
    deployment history is recorded.
  - After the deadline only redeploying the submitted commit is allowed. The
    organisers monitor every deployed site and report changes after the
    deadline to the jury. At the defence you may be asked to show your
    deployment history or the running version on your server.


JUDGING CRITERIA
----------------

Technology decides half of the score: the two technology criteria carry 50%
together.

  Criterion                    Weight   What the jury asks
  ---------------------------  ------   ------------------------------------------
  Technical complexity         25%      Is it a system and not a call to someone
                                        else's model? Is the approach sound and
                                        feasible? Show the architecture, your data
                                        pipeline, quality measured on a test set
                                        you built, speed and cost per request, and
                                        known failure cases.

  Innovation and originality   25%      What is technologically new? The
                                        replication test: what stops another team
                                        with the same LLM tools from rebuilding
                                        this in a weekend? Name your advantage:
                                        data, model, pipeline, integration or
                                        domain knowledge.

  Impact and practicality      25%      Is the problem real and is this the right
                                        solution? Who is the user, who pays, and
                                        what is your evidence? Can it be piloted
                                        in three months within regulation and
                                        existing processes?

  Presentation                 15%      A clear story, a live demo + deployed
                                        website, honest numbers, direct answers,
                                        timing kept.

  Business potential           10%      Budget and ROI: the cost to build and run
                                        a pilot (people, infrastructure, data,
                                        licences), unit economics, and the payback
                                        period with every assumption stated.

Each judge scores every criterion from 0 to 10. The result is the weighted sum,
averaged over the judges. Ties are broken by the sum of the two technology
criteria.


DEFENCE
-------

Each team has 7 minutes to present and 3 minutes for questions (Q&A). Timing
is enforced without exceptions.

  - After 7 minutes the presentation is stopped, mid-sentence if needed. What
    was not shown is not judged.
  - Questions take at most 3 minutes and happen only if the jury has them. The
    jury may address any team member, including about code written with an
    LLM.
  - Technical problems do not stop the clock. Keep a recorded backup of the
    demo.
  - Recommended split of the 7 minutes:
      problem and user   1.0 min
      live demo          2.0 min
      technology         2.0 min
      budget and ROI     1.5 min
      next steps         0.5 min
    The split is a recommendation; the total is not.


HOW TO SUBMIT
-------------

Submit on your team dashboard - all three items, before the deadline:

  1. Prototype - the public https:// link to the deployed web version.
  2. Defence deck - PDF or PowerPoint (.pptx), up to 50 MB.
  3. Repository - a public link (GitHub, GitLab, ...) with the code, a README
     to run it and the list of data sources with their licences.

You can resubmit until the deadline; the latest version counts. At the
deadline the submitted repository is archived and the deployed sites are
monitored for changes.
